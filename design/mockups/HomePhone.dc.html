What is Chiari malformation?

What it is

The lowest part of the cerebellum, the tonsils, extends below the opening at the base of the skull. It's often linked to a lower back part of the skull that is smaller or shaped differently than usual, which crowds the brain. Less often, it develops later in life for other reasons.

Who has it

Most people are born with the skull differences that lead to it. Some never have symptoms; others first notice them as teens or adults.

How it's found

Usually with an MRI scan and a doctor's exam, sometimes by chance.

What it can feel like

The classic sign is a headache at the back of the head after coughing, sneezing or straining.
  • Neck pain
  • Dizziness and trouble with balance
  • Numbness or tingling in hands and feet
  • Weakness or trouble with fine hand movements
  • Trouble swallowing, or voice changes
  • Breathing or sleep problems
  • Ringing in the ears
  • Less often: blurry or shaky vision

Many people with Type I have no symptoms at all.

Common misunderstandings

"It's just a bad headache."

It can affect balance, nerves and the flow of spinal fluid too.

"The measurement tells the whole story."

Lower tonsils make symptoms somewhat more likely, but the measurement alone doesn't reliably predict how someone will feel. Doctors also look at tonsil shape, fluid flow, and whether a syrinx is present.
