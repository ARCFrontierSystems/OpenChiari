# Evidence

Checked November 3, 2026; accuracy-audit fixes added November 4, 2026. Mirrors the "Evidence" tab of the planning doc.

**Opened** means the source page itself was opened and read during the November 4 accuracy checks (CLAUDE.md rule 1a, check 1), not just a search summary. Quotes for opened sources are in the audit reports in `docs/audits/`.

Every medical sentence on the site and every shape in the 3D model is listed here with the source it rests on. **Nothing medical ships without a row in this file.** The exact site wording lives in `docs/COPY.md`.

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Verified | An authoritative source states it directly |
| Partly supported | True with a qualifier; the site wording carries that qualifier |
| Simplified | Accurate in substance but drawn or worded simply for patients; the simplification is named on the site |
| Removed | Not supported; taken off the site |

Preferred sources, in order: NIH/NINDS and NLM, peer-reviewed research, major medical centers, then patient organizations for their own details.

## 3D model shapes

**McRae line:** from the front edge of the foramen magnum (basion) to the back edge (opisthion). Tonsillar descent is the perpendicular distance from that line to the lowest tonsil tip, measured on the midline MRI image or the adjacent one where the tonsils sit lowest ([Barkovich 1986](https://www.ajnr.org/content/ajnr/7/5/795.full-text.pdf), [Radiopaedia](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/chiari-1-malformation)).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tonsil tip | About 1 mm above the line (meta-analysis mean +1.13 mm; reference interval about 7 mm above to 5 mm below, from +6.93 to −4.67 mm; Barkovich 1986 found 8 mm above to 5 mm below in 200 people; lower in children, rises with age) | At least about 5 mm below by definition; model uses about 12 mm, the average in a study of 160 people with Chiari I (12.4 ± 3.7 mm, range 5.0 to 25.4; PMC9834072, opened); often reaches the C1 back arch | [Meta-analysis, PubMed 41081848](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41081848/) (abstract seen in search only; the "3,940 people" count is not confirmed), Barkovich 1986 ([PubMed 3096099](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3096099/), abstract via search), [Polish J Radiol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9834072), [SNI review](https://surgicalneurologyint.com/api/articles/by-slug/definitions-and-treatments-for-chiari-1-malformations-and-its-variants-focused-review) |

**Midline simplification (Simplified):** the tonsils sit just to either side of the midline and often descend unevenly. The model shows them at their lowest point on the midline view, as MRI measures them. The figure caption, the printed visit-prep sheet and the tonsil info card say so ([PMC11371388](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11371388/), opened: "In adult CM1 patients with syringomyelia, most (91%) tonsillar herniations were asymmetrical"; [Tubbs, via springermedizin](https://www.springermedizin.de/sagittal-mri-often-overestimates-the-degree-of-cerebellar-tonsil/10121926)).

**Mockup figure vs built model:** the mockup figure is illustrative, not to scale. Since 2026-11-04 the Simple figure and model take the head, skull, skull base and brain above the foramen magnum from midline cross-sections of the Z-Anatomy atlas (`model/simple/reference/`), and apply the numbers above at and below the foramen magnum (McRae line, tonsils, cord, canal, vertebrae). The realistic model (Phase 2) uses Z-Anatomy throughout.
