## Checked and OK

**Home (desktop, phone, Calm, meta description)**
- "In Chiari malformation, the lowest part of the cerebellum sits below the opening where the brain meets the spinal cord" (all 4 versions). Cleveland: "The lower parts of your cerebellum (the cerebellar tonsils) go into the foramen magnum"; NINDS: "the hole where the brain meets the spinal cord".
- Fig. 1 captions (desktop, phone, Calm): tonsil below the dashed line; shown at its lowest point, where MRI measures it; "Illustration, not a medical image" present in all three. PMC11371388: tonsils "lie dorsal and dorsolateral to the medulla"; asymmetry common. The home figure shows no flow arrows, so "Fluid flow simplified" isn't needed there.
- What it is: tonsils below the foramen magnum (Cleveland); "cerebellum helps control balance and coordination" (NINDS: "the lower part of the brain that controls balance"; Cleveland cerebellum: loss of it causes "balance problems" and loss of "coordination"); smaller back of the skull, crowding (PMC12888059: "the posterior fossa is smaller than expected, causing the cerebellum to ... become crowded"); less often later in life (NINDS: "Congenital CM is much more common than acquired CM").
- Who has it: born with it (Cleveland: "Nearly all CM cases are present at birth"); no symptoms, or first as teens or adults (NINDS: "don't have any symptoms or they don't develop symptoms until they are teenagers or adults").
- How it's found: MRI and exam, sometimes by chance (Cleveland: "Imaging tests ... are the main way"; "physical and neurological exam"; "A provider may spot a CM with imaging tests you get for other reasons").
- "Chiari looks different for everyone, and many people with Type I have no symptoms" and "Many people with Type I have no symptoms at all" (Cleveland: "it affects each person differently"; "Many people don't have symptoms").
- Classic headache (PMC12888059: "typically located at the back of the head and often triggered or worsened by coughing, sneezing, straining"; NINDS).
- Symptoms: neck pain (NINDS); dizziness and balance (NINDS, Cleveland); numbness or tingling (PMC12888059: "numbness or tingling in the arms or legs are common"); weakness or fine hand movements (NINDS: "Problems using hands for small, precise movements"); breathing or sleep problems, sleep apnea (NINDS; Cleveland: "Sleep apnea"); ringing in the ears (NINDS: "tinnitus"); blurry or double vision (Cleveland: "Double or blurred vision"); scoliosis, especially in children (see item 6).
- "It's just a bad headache" (desktop and phone): Cleveland: "structural abnormality", "pressure on your cerebellum and other nerve structures", "block the flow of cerebrospinal fluid".
- "The measurement tells the whole story" (desktop and phone): 5 mm cutoff (NINDS: "at least one-fifth of an inch"); doesn't always match symptoms (PMC12888059: "does not always correlate with symptom burden"); some over 5 mm without symptoms, some under 5 mm with them (PMC11371388: "persons with an MRI diagnosis of CM1 confirmed by TH ≥ 5 mm may be asymptomatic"; patients with CM1-type symptoms "may have tonsillar ectopia ≥5 mm (CM1) or <5 mm (CM0)"); doctors also look at shape, flow, syrinx (PMC9834072: "Different morphological measurements should be made"; PMC12888059: MRI and cine MRI, syringomyelia).
