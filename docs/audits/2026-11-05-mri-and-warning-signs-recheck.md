# Recheck: "Reading your MRI report" and "When to call after surgery" (November 5, 2026)

Independent second check of the text now in `src/i18n/en.json` (`mri`, `prep.warning`) and `src/i18n/es.json` (same keys). The checker did not write the draft, the first audit (`docs/audits/2026-11-05-mri-and-warning-signs-audit.md`) or the translation. Every support quote below was found by text search in the saved copies in `docs/audits/sources/` (file and line number given, relative to that folder; `mri/` = `2026-11-05-mri/`). No blocked site was re-opened and nothing was fetched from the web: the saved copies covered every sentence.

## Short summary

- **Transfer from the first audit is faithful.** Every English sentence in `mri` and `prep.warning` is either the first audit's recommended wording, word for word, or an approved sentence it said to keep. Nothing changed meaning in transfer. (Only difference: the peg-like heading is "Peg-like tonsils" with "pointed tonsils" as the "also" line, instead of "Peg-like or pointed tonsils". Same meaning.)
- **Every medical sentence has support in a saved source**, with one partial exception: the second half of the effacement sentence ("so little or no fluid shows there on the scan") is a reasonable inference but no saved source says it.
- **Blocker (process, not wording):** `docs/EVIDENCE.md` has **no rows** for any sentence on the MRI page or in the warning box, and `docs/COPY.md` has no entry for either. The page itself says "Every sentence on this page comes from a published source, listed in the evidence notes", and the evidence notes page is built from EVIDENCE.md, so that sentence is currently false. The first audit also asked for these rows. Add them before publishing.
- **Safety (warning box), 3 items:**
  1. "being **much** sleepier than usual" is a narrowing qualifier, the kind the first audit removed everywhere else. The sources say "more sleepiness than usual" and "Increased drowsiness".
  2. "**new** problems seeing" uses "new" alone, which the first audit flagged as narrowing for weakness. Mayfield says "New or worsening vision".
  3. The 911 line leaves out several American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) adult warning signs from the same MedlinePlus page: sudden weakness or change in vision, being unable to speak, sudden severe pain, bleeding that won't stop, and fainting. The surgical-team list already covers weakness, vision, speaking and bleeding, so a reader with sudden stroke-like symptoms may call the surgeon's office instead of 911. "If you think it's an emergency, don't wait" helps but doesn't name them.
- **Clarity, 2 items:**
  - "and others use the words the same way" (ectopia) is ambiguous in both languages.
  - "each with a link" is not true for 2 of 13 terms: Posterior fossa and Hydrocephalus have no 3D link.
- **Spanish:** meaning matches the English throughout, and usted is used everywhere. Four places drift from glossary or approved Spanish:
  - "canal de la columna" should be "canal espinal";
  - "herida" should be "incisión";
  - "resonancia cine" should be "cine-RM";
  - two reused approved sentences were re-translated instead of copied.
- **Spanish meaning shifts:** "Estrechez" for "crowding" is a real meaning shift (narrowness, not crowding). The "also" line "fosa posterior pequeña o estrecha" has the same problem. "no lo deje" (medicine) is less clear than it should be for a safety line.
