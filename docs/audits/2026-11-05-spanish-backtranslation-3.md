# Spanish translation: back-translation check of the new surgery and children's text (third pass)

**Date:** 2026-11-05
**Checker:** a fresh agent that did not write the Spanish (CLAUDE.md rule 1a, step 3)
**What was checked:** the newly added Spanish in `src/i18n/es.json`:
`prep.groups` (ids `surgeryBefore`, `surgeryHome`, `surgeryAfter`), `prep.pack`, `prep.child`, all of `children`,
`support.connect`, `explorer.surgery` and `footer.links.children`.
**Method (same as the first two passes):** I first translated every Spanish string back into plain, literal English
*without* opening the English. Only then did I open `src/i18n/en.json` (the approved reference) and compare key by key.
I also checked the Spanish against `docs/SPANISH-GLOSSARY.md`, searched for informal "tú" forms, and read the text as a
Spanish-speaking parent would. The blind back-translation was kept as a working note and was not added to the repo,
because this task allowed only this one file to be written.

## Overall verdict

**Safe to keep published. No high-severity problems.** The Spanish says the same as the English on every medical
point. No hedge was lost: "a menudo" (often), "muchas veces" (many times), "puede" (can), "es posible que" (may),
"quizá" (may), "suelen" (usually) and "a veces" (sometimes) all match. Nothing promises a result, a pain-free stay or a
length of hospital stay. The glossary is followed: "amígdalas cerebelosas" is always in full (3 places), and so are
"duramadre", "líquido cefalorraquídeo" and "médula espinal". The parent text uses "usted" throughout, and "tú" appears
only in the children's story, as intended.

There are **3 medium items** worth fixing soon, and they are all about how a Spanish-speaking parent would read the
words, not about medical facts:

1. **The "put to sleep" tip** keeps the idea but says it awkwardly ("avoid saying 'sleep' the way it is said about
   pets"). A parent could read it as "don't use the word *dormir* at all", which clashes with "special sleep" in the
   same tip.
2. **"Tu persona adulta"** is a word-for-word copy of "your grown-up". It sounds strange in Spanish.
3. **"Pregunte si puede entrar con él al quirófano"** (bring something from home) can be read as "ask if *you* can go
   into the operating room with *your child*". The English is only about the blanket or stuffed animal.

There are also **10 low items**: small naturalness and consistency points. One of them is a real double meaning:
"audífonos" is used for both hearing aids and headphones in the same packing list.

Changes to medical sentences need Matthew's approval (CLAUDE.md rules 1 and 1a), and the Spanish as a whole is still
waiting for the medical translator.

## Answers to the eight points I was asked to look at

| # | Point | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Put to sleep" tip | **Meaning kept, wording needs a fix (medium).** In Spanish, "dormir a" an animal is a common gentle way to say a pet was put down, and "lo van a dormir" is also the everyday way to say "they'll give you anesthesia". So the warning makes sense in Spanish. But the Spanish says to avoid "dormir" in general ("Evite decir 'dormir' como se dice de las mascotas"), which is vague and seems to contradict "un sueño especial" just before it. Naming the exact phrase fixes it (see row C3). |
| 2 | "You didn't do anything to cause it." | **OK.** "Tú no hiciste nada para causarlo." is exact, natural and reassuring. |
| 3 | Hospital stay | **OK, no promise.** "Te quedarás en el hospital hasta que tus médicos digan que estás listo para volver a casa" gives no number of days. The grown-up "will tell you about how long" and will stay "todo lo que pueda" (as much as they can), not "always". Small point: "listo" is the boys' form; see row C19. |
| 4 | "often" / "Many times" | **OK.** Story page 4: "a menudo" (often) and "Muchas veces" (many times) both kept. Explorer steps 3, 4 and 5: "a menudo" kept every time, including "a menudo suben solo en parte" (often rise only partly). Step 1 keeps "puede" (can). |
| 5 | "Tu persona adulta" | **Change (medium).** Understandable, but no Spanish speaker says it; it reads like a translation. The English "your grown-up" is chosen so it fits any family. A natural Spanish phrase that also fits any family: "los adultos que te cuidan" (see row C20). |
| 6 | Glossary terms | **OK.** "amígdalas cerebelosas" in full in `explorer.surgery.intro`, step 1 and step 5 (never "amígdalas" alone). "duramadre" in step 4. "líquido cefalorraquídeo" in steps 1 and 4. "médula espinal" in story page 2 and the coloring key. "siringe" in the follow-up question. "malformación de Chiari tipo I" on the school sheet. No "médula" alone, no "quiste". |
| 7 | Community descriptions and disclaimers | **OK.** All three disclaimers are kept: run by other organizations, not Open Chiari; being listed is not a recommendation; what members share is not checked by Open Chiari and is not medical advice. The privacy warning ("anyone on the internet can read the posts") is kept. "Bobby Jones CSF dice que…" keeps the claim attributed to them, not to Open Chiari. "Members can be as young as 12" is kept. The Spanish adds "en inglés" to the three English-language groups; this is a helpful, intentional addition, like "(en inglés)" elsewhere on the site. |
| 8 | Unnatural wording | A few items, all low except point 5 and row C10. The most noticeable: "audífonos" twice in the packing list with two meanings (row P4), "¿Qué debería hacer que lo llame…?" (row Q12), "pregúntenla juntos" (row C7) and "Jueguen y dibújenlo" (row C11). |

## What was fine

- **Medical meaning.** Every surgery step, story page and school-sheet sentence says the same as the English. No
  "often" became "always", and no "can" became "does".
- **No promises.** No length of stay, no "no pain", no "always with you", no guaranteed patch or neck-bone removal,
  and no promise that the tonsils move back up.
- **Disclaimers and privacy.** "No es consejo médico" on the children's page; "Nada de lo que escriba… se guarda ni
  se envía" in both places; all three community disclaimers and the posting-privacy warning are present.
- **Glossary.** All terms correct (see point 6 above).
- **Formal and informal "you".** The parent text, visit-prep questions and community text use "usted". "Tú" appears
  only in the story pages (pages 2 to 6), as intended. The only switch is the "ustedes" command in rows C7 and C11,
  which is grammatical. It means "you and your child together".
- **Placeholders.** `{n}`, `{total}` and `{name}` are all kept.
