# "What a side-view MRI looks like" (3D roadmap step 5), wording for Matthew's approval (November 5, 2026)

**Status: draft; independent check running.** Matthew: "go ahead with the MRI view" (Nov 5).

**What changed from the plan, and why:**
- **The average adult MRI (MNI152)** has a permissive license, but it ends 14 mm below the skull opening and is blurred.
- **The first volunteer scan** (OpenNeuro ds000030) covers the neck, but it is too grainy at the skull base to place the tonsils or the edges of the opening reliably.
- **What I used instead:** a sharp 0.6 mm T2 scan of a healthy volunteer from OpenNeuro ds004507 (CC0, public domain). The researchers' own manual labels (cord outline, disc points) anchor two of the markers.
- **No overlay on the 3D model.** The scan sits beside numbered markers that use the same numbers as the 3D labels, and each links to the 3D view. An overlay would suggest a millimetre match that a different person's scan can't give.
- **Not marked:** the tonsils' lowest point and the edges of the skull opening. Placing them precisely is a radiologist's call; the medical reviewer could add them later.

## Text

**What a side-view MRI looks like**

> This is a real MRI of a healthy adult volunteer, from a public research collection, cut down the middle like the 3D model. On this kind of scan, called T2, spinal fluid looks bright and the brain and spinal cord look darker. The numbers match the labels in the 3D model.

Markers: 01 Cerebrum · 03 Cerebellum · 04 Fourth ventricle · 05 Brainstem · 08 Spinal cord · 09 Spinal fluid (CSF) · 10 C2 vertebra (the names already used in the 3D Explorer), each with "See it in 3D".

- Caption: MRI of a healthy adult volunteer, side view at the midline (T2). Not a Chiari scan, and not yours: everyone's scan looks a little different. The researchers removed the face before sharing it.
- Note: The lowest point of the tonsils and the edges of the skull opening aren't marked: placing them precisely is a radiologist's job.
- Credit: MRI: Bédard S, Cohen-Adad J. Spinal Cord Head Positions, OpenNeuro ds004507 (public domain, CC0).
- Image description (alt text): Side-view MRI of a healthy adult's head and neck, with numbered markers on the cerebrum, cerebellum, fourth ventricle, brainstem, spinal cord, spinal fluid and the C2 vertebra.
- In the 3D Explorer, under the view: "Compare with a real MRI" (links here).

## Also for approval: coronal and axial cuts in the 3D Explorer (Matthew: "Go ahead with the coronal and axial cuts now")

Under "Cut-away", a new choice: **Side** (as before) · **Front** (coronal, seen from behind) · **Across** (axial, seen from below, the way MRI shows it), with the note: "MRI uses the same three directions: sagittal (side), coronal (front) and axial (across)." The slider's ends read Front · Skull opening · Back, or Lower · Skull opening · Higher. Front and across cuts use the realistic model only: the Simple model is drawn from side-view outlines, so cut any other way it would show shapes that aren't anatomy. The flow and the measure (drawn on the midline) step aside for those two directions.

Spanish: De lado · De frente · Transversal; "La resonancia usa las mismas tres direcciones: sagital (de lado), coronal (de frente) y axial (transversal)."
