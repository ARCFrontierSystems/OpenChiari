## Worth a decision: listings not confirmed in over 2 years

NCT06308367 (betaine) and NCT06268093 (thalidomide), both run by Xuanwu Hospital, Beijing:
- Each still says "Recruiting".
- Their status was last confirmed in February and March 2024.
- Their start dates are still marked "estimated".

ClinicalTrials.gov itself calls a study's status "unknown" when it hasn't been confirmed for 2 years.

**Recommendation:** a standing rule that hides any study whose status hasn't been confirmed in the last 2 years. It's objective and applied automatically every day, so no one has to judge each study.

## Drafts

| Study | Plain summary (what it's trying to learn) | What you'd do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NCT06502431, Analysis of Disturbances in Neurofluid Dynamics in Type I Chiari Malformations (Amiens, France) | Researchers want to understand how spinal fluid and blood move in the head and neck in Chiari I. They use a special MRI that measures flow, comparing adults with and without symptoms to people without Chiari. | Extra flow scans added to an MRI, on one day. For adults 18 to 50 with no past brain or spine surgery and no neurological or heart conditions. |
| NCT06724029, Neurosurgical Outcome Network (hospitals in Italy) | Researchers want to learn what predicts how people do after brain and spine surgery, across many conditions including Chiari I. Hospitals record each person's health, memory and thinking, and mood before surgery and afterwards. | Checks and questionnaires before surgery and at follow-up visits, for up to 5 years. For adults having neurosurgery at a participating hospital in Italy. |
| NCT06308367, The Therapeutic Effect of Betaine in Syringomyelia (Beijing, China) | Researchers want to see whether betaine, a medicine taken by mouth, helps syringomyelia that surgery hasn't helped, and what side effects it has. Some participants take betaine and others take a placebo (a dummy treatment). | Take the medicine or placebo by mouth for 12 weeks, with check-ups before and after. For adults 18 to 80 whose surgery for the syrinx didn't work, or who can't have that surgery. |
| NCT06268093, The Therapeutic Effect of Thalidomide in Syringomyelia (Beijing, China) | Researchers want to see whether thalidomide, a medicine taken by mouth, helps syringomyelia that surgery hasn't helped, and what side effects it has. Everyone in the study takes thalidomide. | Take thalidomide by mouth each night, with check-ups before and about 3 months after treatment. For adults 18 to 80 whose surgery for the syrinx didn't work, or who can't have that surgery. |

None of these studies says people can take part from home, so none is marked "Remote".
