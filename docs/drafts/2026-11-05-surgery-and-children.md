## Revised after the independent audit (this is the wording proposed for approval)

The audit (`docs/audits/2026-11-05-surgery-and-children-audit.md`) passed it with changes. Every change below is the audit's own suggested fix. The first draft further down is kept for the record.

**Picture story**
- Intro: "Read it with your child, and skip any page you like. **Change any words to match what your care team tells you.**"
- Page 2: "Your brain is inside your skull." The words "which keeps it safe" are removed because no opened source says them.
- Page 3: "…reaches down a little too low, **through** the opening where the skull meets the neck. There isn't quite enough room there. **You didn't do anything to cause it.**" This replaces "It is nobody's fault", which no source supports.
- Page 4: "…The surgeon takes away a small piece of bone at the back of the head, **and often a little from the top of the neck. Many times they also add a patch** to make more space." This now matches the drawing and the approved After decompression wording.
- Page 5: "…**You'll stay in the hospital until your doctors say you're ready to go home. Your grown-up will tell you about how long**, and will be with you as much as they can…" This replaces "a few days", which was a promise about time.

**Parent tips**
- "**Ask if the hospital has a child life specialist.** They are trained to talk with children in a comforting way." (KH-READY: "If the hospital has a child life specialist… specially trained to talk to kids in a comforting, informative way.")
- New: "**Accept their feelings.** Let them know it's OK to feel scared, or even mad." (KH-READY: "Let them know that it's OK to feel scared and even mad.")
- New: "**Be ready for changes.** Sometimes surgery is delayed or cancelled because of emergencies." (SK-TALK: "Sometimes surgery may be delayed or canceled because of emergencies.")
- Bring something from home: adds "Ask whether it can go into the operating room." Hospitals differ: SK-AGE says yes, and KH-READY says the parent keeps it during surgery.
- Look after yourself: adds "Talking with a counselor or social worker can help too." (KH-READY: "it can help to talk to a psychologist, social worker, or counselor")

**Visit prep questions**
- Changed:
  - "Which steps will my surgery include? **For example,** will you remove bone only, or also open the covering of the brain and add a patch?"
  - "Which of my symptoms **are likely to** get better, and how long might that take?"
  - "Do I need more scans to check the fluid space, **or my syrinx if I have one**?"
- New in "Before surgery":
  - "When do I need to stop eating and drinking before surgery?"
  - "Will I spend time in intensive care after surgery?" (MLP: "You may have spent some time in the intensive care unit")
  - "For a child: can a parent stay overnight, and be there when they wake up?" (SK-TALK: "you will be able to stay as long as the health-care providers allow")
  - "For a child: can their comfort item go into the operating room?"

**Packing list:** adds "Custody papers, if they apply" (SK-PACK: "Legal custody documents").

**School sheet**
- "In Chiari I, the lowest part of the back of the brain **extends below** the opening at the base of the skull. The surgery makes more room there." This matches evidence row 1.
- New optional box: "Please call me right away if: [from the care team]".

**Captions and credit:** every drawing that shows the tonsils now also says "Tonsils shown at their lowest point, where MRI measures them." The printed school sheet carries the model credit.

**Two source notes corrected (audit finding 12)**
- The KidsHealth follow-up-MRI line is about children who aren't having surgery. The follow-up MRI question rests on STAN (a syrinx may re-form) and is only a question.
- The MedlinePlus blood-thinner line is about restarting medicines after surgery. The medicines question is only a question and states no fact.

## Part 2. Doctor visit prep: surgery questions and a packing list

Three new question groups on the Visit prep page, after the existing ones. None is ticked to start with, so the default sheet doesn't change. On phones they start folded, like the lower groups today.

### Group: "Before surgery"

| Question (as it would appear) | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Why do you recommend surgery, and what are the benefits and risks for me? | CT 4: "If surgery is recommended, ask why it's necessary and what the benefits and risks are." |

### The packing list

A second, separate list on the same page, with its own print button. It prints as its own one-page sheet.

> **Packing for a hospital stay**
>
> Your hospital may send its own list. Follow theirs first.
>
> - Health insurance card and photo ID
> - Your medicines, or a list of them (ask the hospital which to bring)
> - Glasses, hearing aids, a CPAP machine or other equipment you use
> - Toothbrush and other toiletries
> - Comfortable clothes for going home
> - Non-slip slippers
> - Phone and chargers
> - Books, a tablet or games, and headphones
> - For a child: a favorite blanket or stuffed animal
> - For the parent: snacks and a water bottle
> - Your questions sheet from this page
>
> Print button: "Print packing list". Sheet footer: the usual "Made with Open Chiari · openchiari.org · Not medical advice".

Source: SK-PACK (essential items: health card, current medicines, toothbrush and toiletries, comfortable clothes for going home, device chargers; if applicable: health insurance, eyeglasses, hearing aids, CPAP machine; comfort items: non-slip slippers, labelled comfort item, entertainment (books, tablets and games), headphones); SK-AGE ("Bring water and/or snacks for yourself"; "Pack comfort items"). The note "ask the hospital which to bring" is there because hospitals differ: SickKids asks for the medicines themselves, and other hospitals ask you not to bring them. "Follow theirs first" keeps the hospital's own list in charge.

## Part 3. A page for families: "Helping your child through Chiari surgery"

A new page at `/children/` (Spanish `/es/children/`). It is linked from Visit prep and from the site footer. It has four parts.

### 3a. For parents (top of the page)

> **Helping your child through Chiari surgery**
>
> Ideas from children's hospitals for getting a child ready, a picture story to read together, a coloring page, and a sheet for school. Nothing you type here is saved or sent.
>
> **Getting ready together**
> - **Be honest, in words your child knows.** Younger children usually need fewer details than older kids. Teens may want to know more and to help make decisions.
> - **Say why.** The surgery is to help a part of their body do its job.
> - **Call it a special sleep.** The medicine for surgery gives a special sleep, so they won't see, hear or feel anything, and the care team wakes them when it's over. Avoid "put to sleep": some children know it from pets that never woke up.
> - **Don't promise "no pain" or "no needles."** Instead, say the nurses and doctors have medicine and ways to help.
> - **It's fine to say "I don't know."** Write the question down and ask the care team together.
> - **Bring something from home.** A favorite blanket or stuffed animal can help.
> - **Play and draw it.** Acting out a hospital visit with toys, or drawing it, can show you how your child feels.
> - **Ask for a child life specialist.** Many children's hospitals have one, trained to help children understand and cope.
> - **Look after yourself too.** Children pick up on how you feel. Accept help when it's offered.

### 3b. A picture story to read together

Six short pages, each with a figure from the site (the same midline drawing, without labels or measurements). There's a "Print the story" button. The story speaks to the child. The parent can skip any page.

> **1.** This is a picture of the inside of a head and neck, as if we could look in from the side. *(Figure: Typical)*
>
> **2.** Your brain is inside your skull, which keeps it safe. Near the back is a part called the cerebellum. It helps you balance and move smoothly. Your brain and spinal cord are cushioned by a clear liquid. *(Figure: Typical)*
>
> **3.** In Chiari, the bottom of the cerebellum reaches down a little too low, into the opening where the skull meets the neck. There isn't quite enough room there. It is nobody's fault. *(Figure: Chiari I)*
>
> **4.** Doctors can make more room. During surgery you'll be in a special sleep from medicine, so you won't see, hear or feel anything. The surgeon takes away a small piece of bone at the back, and often adds a patch to make more space. *(Figure: After decompression)*
>
> **5.** When you wake up, the back of your head and neck may feel sore. Your nurses and doctors have medicine to help. You'll stay in the hospital for a few days, and your grown-up will be with you as much as they can. You can bring your favorite blanket or stuffed animal. *(No figure: a plain card)*
>
> **6.** Then you'll rest at home for a while before going back to school. You can ask questions anytime. Every question is a good one. *(No figure)*
>
> Caption on every figure: "Illustration, not a medical image." The usual credit is printed at the end of the printed story.
