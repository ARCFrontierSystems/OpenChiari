## Part 1. What happens in surgery, step by step (3D Explorer)

**Where:** a panel under the parts list in the Chiari I and After decompression views (in the bottom sheet on phones).

**How it works:**
- **Play** walks through five steps, about 7 seconds each. **Back** and **Next** step by hand, and **Show the usual view** ends it.
- In Calm mode, or with reduced motion, nothing moves by itself: Play shows step 1, then Next and Back.
- Screen readers hear each step. The flow marks and the measure bracket are hidden while it plays.

**What changes in the model at each step:**
1. **Before surgery:** the After decompression view drawn as Chiari I. Low tonsils, crowded fluid space, all bone in place.
2. **The incision:** a dark red line on the back skin outline. It runs from the level of the inion (where the tentorium meets the back of the skull) down to the level of the C2 spinous process, about 7 cm in the model.
3. **Bone removed:** the bone at the back of the skull opening and the back of C1 shown as removed (the existing dashed "bone removed" marking).
4. **The patch:** the dural patch appears (the existing dashed patch line).
5. **Afterward:** the full After decompression view: the re-formed fluid space and the partly risen tonsils. This is the existing approved figure.

Steps 1 to 4 reuse the approved shapes. The only new drawing is the incision line, labeled "approximate".

**Text:**

> **What happens in surgery, step by step**
>
> Surgeons don't all do the same operation. Some remove bone only, and some also shrink the tonsils. Your surgeon can tell you which steps apply to you.
>
> 1. **Before surgery.** In Chiari I, the tonsils extend below the opening at the base of the skull. This can crowd the space where spinal fluid flows.
> 2. **The incision.** The person is asleep under anesthesia. The surgeon makes a cut down the middle of the back of the head and upper neck, and moves the muscles aside to reach the bone.
> 3. **Bone removed.** A small piece of bone is removed at the back of the skull opening, and often the back of the first neck bone (vertebra), to make more room.
> 4. **The patch.** Often the surgeon also opens the brain's tough covering (the dura) and sews in a patch, like letting out a waistband, to make more room for spinal fluid. Then the muscles and skin are closed.
> 5. **Afterward.** On scans after surgery, the fluid space behind the cerebellum is often larger, and the tonsils often rise only partly.
>
> Caption: "Animation simplified. The incision line is approximate. Illustration, not a medical image · Tonsils shown at their lowest point, where MRI measures them"

## Part 2. Connect with others (Support page)

A new section between the organizations and "Other ways to help". The links go straight to each community. Open Chiari adds no social features.

> **Connect with others**
>
> Talking with people who have been through it can help. These communities are run by other organizations, not by Open Chiari. What members share there is their own experience, not medical advice, and Open Chiari doesn't check it.
>
> - **Chiari Support (Ben's Friends)**, an online forum. A support forum for patients, families and friends, part of Ben's Friends, a network of patient communities for rare diseases. Members can be as young as 12. *Visit Chiari Support* (chiarisupport.org)
> - **Bobby Jones CSF Support Community**, an online community on Inspire. An online community for patients and families living with Chiari malformation, syringomyelia and related disorders. You choose how anonymous you are. *Visit the community* (inspire.com)
> - **ASAP support groups**, by video, phone and in person. Monthly support groups over Zoom, monthly phone support groups, and a list of local groups that meet in person. *Find a group* (asap.org/find-support)
> - **FEMACPA (Spain)**, in Spanish. The Spanish federation of Chiari patient associations. It offers support after diagnosis. *Visit FEMACPA* (femacpa.com)
>
> Before you post, look at each site's privacy settings. Some posts can be seen by anyone. Links checked November 2026.
