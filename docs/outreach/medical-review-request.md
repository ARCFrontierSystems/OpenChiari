# Asking for a medical review (draft for Matthew to send)

Phase C of the plan Matthew approved on Nov 5. **Nothing here is sent automatically.** Matthew sends it himself, from his own email, to people he chooses.

## Who to ask, in order

1. **Patient organizations with medical advisory boards.** They already point patients to resources, and a review fits what they do. Start with the three on the Support page:
   - Bobby Jones Chiari & Syringomyelia Foundation;
   - Conquer Chiari;
   - ASAP (American Syringomyelia & Chiari Alliance Project).

   Use each one's contact page and ask that the message reach their medical advisory board.
2. **Researchers who publish on Chiari I imaging or education.** The authors of the papers in EVIDENCE.md are a natural list, for example the review PMC12888059 and the study of 160 people. Write to the corresponding author named on the paper.
3. **A neurosurgery or neuroradiology resident or nurse educator you know personally.** A personal connection often answers fastest. A scoped review from a clinician still counts, as long as the site states their role honestly.

Ask a few at a time, not everyone at once. One good reviewer is enough to start.

## What to ask for: a small, clear job

Keep it to about **one to two hours**. Ask the reviewer to:
- read the medical sentences, which are listed with their sources in the evidence notes (openchiari.org/evidence/);
- look at the four 3D views (typical, Chiari I, with syrinx, after decompression), the surgery steps and "Reading your MRI report";
- say what's wrong, misleading or missing. A list of comments is enough; they don't need to rewrite anything.

**What they get:**
- their name, role and the date on the About page, saying exactly what they reviewed ("reviewed the medical text and 3D views on [date]");
- or no name at all, if they prefer. In that case the page says "reviewed by a [role]" only if they agree to that.

**What we never do:**
- say "medically reviewed" about anything they didn't look at;
- keep the claim after the content changes a lot. The review date stays next to it, and big changes are flagged for a new look.

## The email (fill in the parts in [brackets])

> **Subject:** Request: a short medical review of a free Chiari education site
>
> Dear [name],
>
> I'm Matthew Borgfeld. I have Chiari malformation and had surgery for it at age 5. I've built Open Chiari (openchiari.org), a free, open-source site that helps people newly diagnosed with Chiari I, and parents of children having surgery, see what's happening in 3D and prepare better questions for their doctors. It has no ads, no accounts and no tracking.
>
> Every medical sentence on the site is tied to a published source, and these are listed in the evidence notes (openchiari.org/evidence/). So far the content has been checked against those sources with AI-assisted reviews, but **not yet by a medical professional**, and the site says so. I'd like to change that.
>
> Would you, or someone on your [medical advisory board / team], be willing to spend an hour or two reviewing:
> - the medical text (listed with sources in the evidence notes);
> - the four 3D views and the surgery steps;
> - the new "Reading your MRI report" page?
>
> Comments in any form are welcome. With your permission, the About page would credit you by name and role, with the date and exactly what you reviewed. Or it would credit no one by name, if you'd prefer.
>
> The 3D model is also free for your own use (CC BY-SA 4.0), with a citable copy on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23983232), if it would help your patients or members.
>
> Thank you for considering it.
>
> Matthew Borgfeld
> openchiari.org
