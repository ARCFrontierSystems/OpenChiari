# A 5-person usability test (script for Matthew)

Phase C of the plan Matthew approved on Nov 5. **The aim:** find out whether people who are new to the site can do the things it's for, and where they get stuck. Five people find most of the big problems.

**Why this way:** it needs no tracking, no accounts and no data stored on the site. You watch and take notes, and that's all.

## Who

- **Five people:**
  - two or three who have, or recently had, a Chiari diagnosis;
  - one or two parents of a child with Chiari;
  - ideally one who isn't comfortable with computers.
- **Where to find them:** the Chiari communities on the Support page, or people you know.
- **Ages:** a mix, if you can. At least two should use a phone, since that's how most people will visit.
- **Not anyone who has seen the site before,** if possible.

## How (about 20 minutes each)

- Video call with screen sharing, or sitting next to them.
- Ask them to **think out loud**: "Say what you're looking at and what you're thinking."
- **Don't help.** If they're stuck for over a minute, say "What would you try next?" If they're still stuck, move to the next task and note it.
- **Write down** where they hesitate, what they tap that doesn't do what they expected, and the words they use.

Start with: "This is a website about Chiari malformation. I'm testing the website, not you, so there are no wrong answers. Please think out loud."

## The tasks (read each one out; don't show them the page names)

1. **First look (10 seconds on the home page, then look away):** "What is this site for? Who is it for?"
2. **Basics:** "Imagine you were just told you have Chiari. Find out what that means." *(Do they find the tour or the 3D Explorer? Do they understand the tonsils and the line?)*
3. **Your report:** "Your MRI report says your tonsils are '7 mm below the foramen magnum'. Find out what that means." *(The MRI report page: do they find it from Home?)*
4. **3D:** "Show me the difference between a typical brain and Chiari in the 3D model." *(Watch the control panel: is anything confusing? This answers whether to simplify the Explorer.)*
5. **Prepare:** "You have a first appointment with a neurosurgeon next week. Make a list of questions to bring." *(Do they use a quick-start? Do they print?)*
6. **Explain:** "Your sister doesn't understand what Chiari is. Send her something that explains it." *(Explain It)*
7. **Surgery:** "Surgery is planned. What will happen, and when should you call the doctor after you go home?"
8. **Spanish (only for Spanish speakers):** "Switch the site to Spanish."

## At the end, ask

- "What was most useful?"
- "What was confusing or annoying?"
- "Was there anything you expected to find but didn't?"
- "Would you send this site to someone? Who?"
- "Did anything feel scary or upsetting?" *(This matters for a medical site.)*

## Afterwards

Send your notes to Claude in any form, even rough. Claude will:
- group them into problems, ordered by how many people hit each one;
- suggest fixes;
- check each fix against the site's rules, so medical text still gets a source and your approval.

Nothing anyone says gets published.
